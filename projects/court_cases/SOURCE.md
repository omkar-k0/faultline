## Data

All seed data is **synthetic**, generated by `generator/generate.py` with a fixed seed (`20261118`). Re-running it reproduces the committed CSV files byte for byte (a test checks this).

Every court, judge, lawyer, party and case is fictional. Names are codes ("District Court 04 (Delhi)", "Presiding Officer 017", "Advocate 042", "Company Party 0311") so none refers to a real person or court. The appeal limitation periods in `appeal_deadlines` are a made-up rule set for this project, not Indian law.

## What it models

A court case lifecycle: courts, judges, lawyers, parties, cases, filings with court fees, fee payments, hearings (most adjourned) and interim and final orders. Times are recorded as local Indian Standard Time without a zone, as a court register would; staging converts them to UTC with each court's offset. That makes timezone faults realistic.

| Layer | Models |
|---|---|
| staging | stg_courts, stg_judges, stg_lawyers, stg_parties, stg_cases, stg_hearings, stg_filings, stg_orders, stg_fee_payments |
| intermediate | int_case_hearings, int_filing_fees, int_case_filings, int_case_final_orders |
| marts | fct_cases, fct_hearings, dim_courts, dim_lawyers, court_backlog_monthly, fee_reconciliation, appeal_deadlines |

`fee_reconciliation` lists filings whose fee is not exactly paid. In the clean data every fee is paid in full, so it is empty until a fault breaks the fees.
